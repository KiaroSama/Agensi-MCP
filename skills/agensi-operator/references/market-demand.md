# Market Demand: Choose a Paid-Value Hypothesis, Not a Popular Label

Research baseline refreshed2026-10-10: public catalogue, task-specific competing offers, recent-popular response and request board. This is original synthesis; creator identities/directories are deliberately omitted. Sources are grouped at the end. Actual sales/conversion/revenue are not public in the inspected evidence, so "more likely to sell" below means a defensible hypothesis to validate, not a sales leaderboard.

## The useful market conclusion

A stronger paid proposition is **one recurring costly job, for a recognizable buyer, ending in a usable verified artifact**, with less setup/review than the free alternative. A weak proposition is the same generic prompt with a premium title, a large role bundle or unsupported authority claims. The market evidence supports investigating this distinction; it does not prove that wording alone causes purchases.

Recurring developer/writing tasks have conspicuous accumulated uptake. Paid interest is visible in structured datasets, incident analysis, upgrade planning, specialized design implementation and context/reasoning workflows. However, closely named same-price competitors can show little uptake. Older exposure, promotion, audience, trust and distribution confound quality/price interpretation. A clear workflow is necessary for selection, not sufficient for demand.

## Which offers deserve validation first?

These are **research priorities**, not proven profitability. Choose within the owner's competence and actual buyer access; do not invent expertise merely because a market looks attractive.

| Priority / offer family | Concrete paid-worthy output | Why worth investigating | Main reason it might not sell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start here: evidence-driven developer QA | A specific stack's upgrade/incident/test-eval packet with traceable inputs, changed files, checks and rollback | Repeated technical pain and paid uptake exist; reviewable artifacts differentiate from vague advice | Generic review/planning already has free substitutes; unseen correctness, proprietary data and maintenance burden |
| Start here if delivery expertise exists: specialized implementation | Editable builder components, working migration patch or validated export with before/after evidence | Completed usable artifact has different value from another implementation plan | Setup/tool permissions and external write risk; rights/compatibility/fidelity can break the promise |
| Validate with a known buyer: document/data operations | Receipts/invoices/exports→reconciled monthly records plus exceptions and audit trail, draft-only by default | A fulfilled public request explicitly describes this repeat admin job; time/error cost is tangible | One request is not market size; privacy, bank/email access and messy real inputs create friction; similar paid offers show weak public uptake |
| Validate a narrow niche: brand/creative consistency | Calibrated voice/style, batch consistency checks, platform-ready assets and flagged deviations | Recent interest in bounded creative/consistency tasks; reusable specs can add value beyond rewriting | Generic humanizers are crowded/free; claim of "viral/award-winning" adds no proof; asset rights and subjective evaluation |
| Explore only with data advantage: specialist source-backed workflows | A narrow language/locale/domain question answered with live authoritative evidence and an exportable result | Recent public interest includes localized lookup and research jobs; clear source/task fit | Public APIs/free instruction access may remove willingness to pay; live accuracy/support costs, regulated claims |

Prefer the first family only when the buyer actually repeats that job and the proposed gap exists. Do not default to building a generic code reviewer: popularity there also means abundant free competition.

## Evidence ladder

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| User describes repeated pain with current workaround | Problem exists for that person | Whole-market demand or payment |
| Public request with bounty/fulfilled status | Expressed task interest, possible willingness-to-pay signal | Verified bounty payment, fulfilled quality or recurring purchases |
| Paid listing installs | Observed paid-offer uptake | Number of paid transactions, successful uses, realized price/revenue |
| Free installs/reviews | Awareness/adoption clues and qualitative fit evidence | Conversion to paid, paid price ceiling |
| Actual approved paid pilot/transaction | Real willingness to pay in that specific context | Durable retention/large market without further cohorts |

## Opportunity workflow

### 1. Define the job and existing workaround

Write one sentence:

> For [buyer], when [trigger], turn [available input] into [accepted artifact], reducing [observed time/error/risk], without [unapproved action or unsupported promise].

Record current workaround (ordinary prompt, free skill, manual tool, employee/service), frequency, who approves/pays and the evidence. If frequency/buyer/problem is unknown, research or interview under the user's approval before declaring a market. Do not invent hours saved or ROI.

**Done:** one buyer-job and one real alternative, with uncertainty marked.

### 2. Check both demand and saturation

Search problem terms, output terms and integration terms; compare paid and free alternatives. Read details for a strong-uptake paid offer, a plausible low-uptake competitor and a free baseline. Capture deliverable, input, limitations, price, prerequisites, last meaningful version and proof. Cluster duplicates by the job—not merely name or creator.

**Done:** a small comparison exposes either a real gap or no defensible differentiation. Another same-topic title is not a gap.

### 3. Define the gap that a buyer can verify

Potential gaps: exact niche/locale/format support; source-to-result traceability; usable implementation instead of plan; reliable edge/failure handling; privacy-preserving local operation; deterministic export; much less setup/manual rework; maintained version-specific checks.

Weak gaps: more pages, more roles, "Pro", arbitrary checklist counts, named-person persona, impressive percentages without a baseline. If a plain prompt already meets the contract, recommend using it instead of inventing a paid skill.

**Done:** one differentiator and how a buyer would recognize it in a sample.

### 4. Test a narrow prototype and the free baseline

Use supplied/approved representative inputs. For technical/data tasks, include a messy/error case and validate files/rows/facts directly. For writing/design, preserve meaning/constraints and use a buyer-aligned rubric plus real review. Hold some examples out of revision; compare the same host/model/tool/input conditions. Use [skill-optimization.md](skill-optimization.md) for the actual loop.

**Done:** concrete output advantage or a documented failure/no advantage. A listing scan or schema-valid SKILL.md is not outcome evidence.

### 5. Decide invest, narrow or stop

Use gates rather than a made-up commercial score:

- **Invest in validation:** buyer pain is observed; free alternative leaves a specific gap; prototype can show it; setup/privacy/support are feasible; required expertise/rights exist.
- **Narrow/revise:** pain is plausible but too broad, differentiator vague or output only a plan; validate one subjob before broadening.
- **Stop/defer:** no actual buyer/task, no advantage over free/simple prompting, unavailable data/tools, unacceptable risk, unverifiable compliance or cost beyond plausible value.

Research approval covers analysis only. Buyer outreach, evaluations, spending, pilots, publication and product creation beyond the requested internal guide require their own approval. Suggest the test; do not perform an external action silently.

## Pricing and delivery model

Choose model from what the buyer receives, not content-protection convenience:

- **Free:** a complete bounded diagnostic/sample that solves its promised job. Make limits honest; do not label a broken teaser useful. Free uptake does not establish a converting funnel.
- **One-time:** reusable agent-delivered access to a workflow/output contract with stable value; not downloadable buyer files. Clarify update/support/version scope and license. Check current minimum/offer rather than outdated creator articles.
- **Subscription:** credible ongoing value (maintained current data/service, repeat hosted work or continuous updates), not simply a static prompt behind monthly payment. Agensi's skill subscriptions are cloud-only; validate privacy/capabilities/cost before recommending this delivery.

Set a **price hypothesis**, not an optimum: compare comparable current offers and incremental value; factor setup, external API charges, support and refund risk. Use actual buyer/transaction tests for willingness to pay. No public install×price×commission revenue estimate. A service/API connector advertised as a free skill can still have significant run costs.

Older creator pricing advice conflicts with current minimum/bundle Terms; current offer and [policies.md](policies.md) control. The October12 paid minimum is $1; below$5 uses credits only. Historic $5-minimum advice is superseded.

## Worked opportunity brief — internal example, not a product submission

**Candidate:** Evidence-Linked Upgrade Packet for one supported TypeScript project stack.

**Buyer/job:** Maintainer planning a version upgrade from a lockfile and changelog, needing an ordered actionable patch/check/rollback packet.

**Market basis:** Related paid planning uptake exists, but similar low-uptake offers and free advice show saturation. Actual paid transactions unknown.

**Gap hypothesis:** Not another generic roadmap; every proposed change maps to actual usage/files and cited breaking-change evidence. Separate mechanical edits from decisions; include runnable project checks and explicit blocked cases.

**Prototype:** One supplied safe repository+upgrade target; deliver usage map, limited patch suggestions, check results/rollback and unresolved decisions. No auto-update/deploy without approval.

**Validation:** Against ordinary prompting/free baseline, verify cited APIs/versions, correct changed-file scope, usable test instructions and absence of invented compatibility claims; compare buyer rework/time only if actually measured. Hold out a second repository/missing-prerequisite scenario.

**Decision:** Validate if expertise/current source access/representative repository exist; otherwise narrow to analysis-only or defer. Pricing remains hypothesis until buyer evidence; do not promise it will outsell established offers.
